	Explain the concepts of density-reachable and density-connected points in DBSCAN, and state which one is symmetric and why symmetry is important for cluster definition.

Density-connected is symmetric, stating that two points $p$ and $q$ are density-connected if there is a point $o$ from which both $p$ and $q$ are density-reachable. The symmetric notion of density-connectedness is necessary to properly define a cluster as a maximal set of density-connected points.

	Manhattan distance is the $L_1$ norm ($p=1$), also called the city block distance. Euclidean distance is the $L_2$ norm ($p=2$). SGD updates parameters based on the gradient of a single training example, making it faster and more lightweight per update. SGD can start making progress right away, often getting $\theta$ close to the minimum faster than BGD, though it is generally more sensitive to the learning rate $\alpha$.

	The Elbow Method plots the SSE (Sum of Squared Errors) as a function of $K$. As $K$ increases, SSE naturally decreases. The optimal $K$ is typically found at the point where diminishing returns are observed—where the error starts decreasing at a much slower rate, forming a "knee" or "elbow" in the curve.

	Define the Apriori Property (Anti-monotonicity) of frequent patterns and explain its role in the Apriori algorithm.
	The Apriori Property states that any subset of a frequent itemset must also be frequent. Its role is the pruning principle: if an itemset fails to be frequent (is not "large"), then all of its supersets must also be infrequent, allowing the algorithm to avoid generating or testing those supersets, thus speeding up the search.
	SA 52
	Freq. Patterns
	Define Lift and explain what values of Lift $> 1$, Lift $= 1$, and Lift $< 1$ indicate.
	Lift measures the correlation between two itemsets $A$ and $B$, calculated as $\text{Lift} = \frac{P(A \cup B)}{P(A) P(B)}$. If Lift $= 1$, $A$ and $B$ are independent; if Lift $> 1$, they are positively correlated; and if Lift $< 1$, they are negatively correlated.

	Null Invariance is the property that a measure's value does not change based on the number of transactions containing neither item ("null transactions"). Lift fails to satisfy this property, which means it can be misleading (e.g., suggesting a rule is strong when it may be neutral).

20. Explain the difference between Support and Confidence measures for an association rule $X \to Y$ using probability notation.

   * Reasoning: Support ($s$) measures how frequently $X$ and $Y$ occur together in the dataset, calculated as the probability $P(X \cup Y)$,. Confidence ($c$) measures the conditional probability that a transaction contains $Y$ given that it contains $X$, calculated as $P(Y|X)$,.
   21. Why might the rule "Buy coffee $\to$ buy milk" be found as a frequent rule with high confidence, yet be considered misleading or uninteresting?

      * Reasoning: This rule can be misleading because Support and Confidence are not good measures to indicate correlations. It might be frequent/high confidence if $P(\text{Milk})$ is very high overall (high prior probability), even if $P(\text{Milk}|\text{Coffee})$ is not significantly higher than $P(\text{Milk})$ alone. If, for example, 85% of customers buy milk, and the rule confidence is 80%, the rule suggests a negative or neutral correlation,.
      22. What does it mean for a measure of interestingness to be Null Invariant, and why is this a desirable property?

         * Reasoning: Null Invariance means that the measure's value does not change with the number of transactions that contain neither of the items we are interested in (null transactions). This is desirable because total transaction numbers fluctuate daily, and Null Invariance guarantees stability in the measure and ensures the measure is not sensitive to total transaction fluctuations.
         23. Describe the function of the Apriori pruning step, citing the underlying property that makes it work.

            * Reasoning: The Apriori pruning step ensures that if any itemset is found to be infrequent (fails to be 'large'), all of its supersets should not be generated or tested. This is based on the Apriori Property (Anti-monotonicity), which states that any subset of a frequent itemset must be frequent.
            24. When comparing two itemsets, $A$ and $B$, why is the Jaccard similarity (Coherence) said to prefer balanced patterns while the Kulczynski measure favors skewed patterns?

               * Reasoning: In the context of frequent patterns, Coherence (Jaccard) prefers balanced patterns (e.g., two comparable collaborators), while Kulczynski tends to favor skewed patterns (e.g., advisor-advisee relationships).
Topic 6: Unsupervised Learning (Clustering) (10 Questions)
               25. Outline the steps of the K-means clustering algorithm (Lloyd's algorithm) until convergence.

                  * Reasoning: 1. Initialization: Pick $K$ points at random and set them as initial centroids,. 2. Assignment: Assign each data point to the nearest centroid (according to Euclidean distance),. 3. Update: Compute new cluster centroids as the mean of all points assigned to that cluster,. 4. Repeat: Iterate steps 2 and 3 while cluster assignments don't change or until a convergence criterion (like minimal SSE change) is met,.
                  26. Explain what the SSE (Sum of Squared Errors) measures in K-means and how the algorithm minimizes it.

                     * Reasoning: SSE measures the total error of approximating every data point by its assigned centroid,. It is defined as the sum of squared Euclidean distances between each point and its assigned centroid. K-means minimizes SSE using alternating optimization, where subsequent assignments and mean calculations ensure the SSE decreases or stays the same in every iteration,.
                     27. Describe K-means++ initialization and explain why it is an improvement over simple random initialization.

                        * Reasoning: K-means++ chooses the initial centroids such that subsequent centroids are far away from already chosen centroids. It starts by choosing a random point as $c_1$. It then selects the next centroid $c_2$ with probability proportional to the squared distance $D(x_j, c_1)^2$. This method helps avoid poor local optima resulting from simple random placement.
                        28. Explain two limitations of the K-means clustering algorithm.

                           * Reasoning: 1. K-means is sensitive to noisy data and outliers because the mean (centroid) can be substantially distorted by extreme values. 2. It requires specifying $K$ (the number of clusters) in advance. 3. It is only applicable to objects in a continuous n-dimensional space.
                           29. When performing hierarchical clustering, compare Single Linkage and Complete Linkage in terms of how distance between clusters is defined.

                              * Reasoning: Single Linkage computes the distance between two clusters based on the smallest distance (minimum pairwise distance) between an element in one cluster and an element in the other, tending to find local patterns,. Complete Linkage computes the distance based on the largest distance between elements, tending to find clusters based on global proximities,.
                              30. What is the goal of density-based clustering methods like DBSCAN, and what two parameters do they require?

                                 * Reasoning: The motivation is to discover clusters of arbitrary shapes and handle noise. DBSCAN requires two parameters: Eps (Maximum radius of the neighborhood) and MinPts (Minimum number of points in an Eps-neighborhood).
                                 31. Explain the meaning of a core point and a border point in DBSCAN.

                                    * Reasoning: A core point is a point $q$ whose Eps-neighborhood contains at least $MinPts$ number of points. A border point is a non-core point that is density-reachable from a core point but does not satisfy the core point condition.
                                    32. Describe two different ways to determine the optimal number of clusters $K$ when ground truth labels are unavailable.

                                       * Reasoning: 1. Elbow Method (Knee Finding): Plot the SSE (Sum of Squared Errors) versus $K$. The optimal $K$ is suggested where the decrease in SSE shows diminishing returns (the "knee" of the curve),. 2. Silhouette Coefficient: Calculate the average Silhouette Coefficient for different values of $K$. The value of $K$ that maximizes the Silhouette Coefficient (which measures cohesion vs. separation) often indicates good clustering quality.
                                       33. Define Cluster Homogeneity and Cluster Completeness, and explain why they are considered fundamental restrictions for evaluation metrics.

                                          * Reasoning: Cluster Homogeneity requires that clusters should not mix items belonging to different categories (the purer, the better),. Cluster Completeness requires that items belonging to the same category should be grouped in the same cluster,. These are the two most basic restrictions any clustering evaluation metric must hold.
                                          34. Why is the optimal solution for K-means clustering considered NP-Hard?

                                             * Reasoning: Finding the optimal partition of $N$ objects into $K$ clusters such that the SSE is globally minimized is an NP-Hard problem. This is because exhaustively enumerating all possible partitions is computationally infeasible. K-means uses a heuristic method (Lloyd's algorithm) to find a local optimum instead,.
